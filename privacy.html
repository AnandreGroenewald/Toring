Privacy policy

Version: 2026-10-13 · Afrikaans

Stapel is a free game that knows as little about you as possible. Here we explain exactly what we store, why, where, for how long, and what your rights are. It complies with South Africa’s Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA). This is a translation of the Afrikaans policy; if the two ever differ, the Afrikaans one applies.

In short: players don’t have to give anything, and your game data stays on your own device, unless you choose to sign in with Google to save your progress (it is never needed to play; then we keep your progress and a number made from your Google account, not your name or email; you can delete it at any time: section 2d). We only count anonymously how often sponsors are seen, how high the daily towers get and how often results are shared; a live Challenger Series match keeps your (optional) nickname and heights for at most 7 days; and the daily leaderboard shows your nickname and today’s height (30 days; you can hide yourself) (section 2). Only advertisers (sponsors) give personal information, and only what is needed to invoice and contact them. This applies to the website and to the Stapel app on Google Play (Android), which is the same game.

2. Players: what we collect and what we don’t

  • You don’t need to create an account or type in any information to play Stapel. Signing in with Google is optional (section 2d).
  • Your results, streak, best height and settings are stored only on your own device (see section 11) and are not sent to us. The exceptions are the anonymous count and the daily leaderboard below, and, only if you choose to sign in with Google, your saved progress (section 2d).
  • We use no analytics or tracking services from other companies, no advertising networks and no cookies to follow you. We do count anonymously how often sponsors are seen and how the daily tower goes; exactly what is below.
  • When you share your result, it goes through your own app (such as WhatsApp). We don’t receive it; we only count anonymously that the share button was tapped (section 2a).
  • The game fetches the list of sponsors, and how many free tries the Daily Tower has a day, from our website or server. These are anonymous requests without any information about you. As with any website, the services that send out the files (section 6) technically see your IP address; we don’t store it or use it to identify you.
  • Stapel is meant for players aged 13 and older. We never ask for a player’s real name, email or other contact details. The only player information we keep for a while is: for the daily leaderboard your nickname, your random player number and your result (height, blocks, playing time and tries), for 30 days; and after a live match a recording with your nickname and heights, for 7 days. After that it is deleted automatically (below). If you choose to sign in with Google, we also keep your saved progress until you delete it (section 2d). Please don’t use your full name as a nickname.

Anonymous counts

When a game ends, and when you tap a share button on the results, the game sends one small message to our server, but only when our server is on. It is just numbers with a date:

  • What is counted: the date; whether it was the Daily Tower or a practice game; how many times each sponsor’s name was on a block you dropped; which sponsor’s billboard stood on the island; whether the ad was shown on the menu; and how many times the share buttons on the results were tapped (just a number per day and kind of game).
  • The Daily Tower: your height (in steps of 0.5 m) is added to a list with everyone’s results of that day. That lets the game say “You did better than 72% of players today”. The line appears only once at least 10 people have played. The result is sent at most once a day per device.
  • What is never sent or stored: your name, email address, IP address, device ID, cookies, the time of day or anything else that tells one player from another. We only store totals (“there were 412 games on 6 October”), not a list of who played, and they can’t be linked to you. So this is not personal information.
  • Preventing abuse: to stop one computer from inflating the numbers, the server remembers your network address for at most an hour in its memory, as a key that changes every day (it can’t be turned back into an address). It is not stored in the database or the logs.
  • Why: so that we can tell sponsors how often their name was seen (they get a monthly report), and to be able to show the daily comparison.
  • How long: these totals are kept for about 13 months and then deleted automatically.
  • Is our server off, or are you without internet? Then nothing is sent and the game works as usual.

Challenger Series (head-to-head)

  • Against the computer or with a link: nothing is sent to us. A challenge link you share yourself contains your nickname and your tower’s height second by second. Whoever gets the link can see it.
  • Live matches: while you play, the server passes your nickname, your player card (your looks, your rank and the blocks you chose) and your height on to your opponent, and in a coin match how many coins each of you puts in; in Blok vir Blok (Block by Block) also every block you drop and how the tower stands afterwards, and in both modes the emojis you send (six fixed pictures, no written messages). After the match it keeps a recording (the nickname and the heights, nothing else) for at most 7 days, so that a player who finds nobody to play against can play against a real match. After that it is deleted automatically.
  • A friend’s link: whoever opens a room link (?kamer=) sees the nickname of the player who invites and the kind of match. If they tap “Sorry, busy right now” (or, for a coin match, “Let them know: not enough coins”), the player who invited gets their nickname (and, for a coin match, that they have too few coins for it); an older Stapel that opens a coin-match link passes its nickname on too, and the player who invited reads that it doesn’t know coin matches yet. The room keeps at most 5 such answers until that player’s game is back, and forgets everything when the room closes (at most a few minutes after the match).
  • Back after a dropped connection: the game sends a random code that only works for that one room, so you get your own seat back. It is used nowhere else and disappears with the room.
  • What is never stored: your IP address, device ID, cookies or anything else that points back to you. Your nickname is optional; without one you play as “Bouer” (Builder) with a number.

Daily leaderboard

  • What is sent: when you have finished the Daily Tower, the game sends your nickname (or “Bouer” with a number if you didn’t choose one), your tower’s height, how many blocks you dropped, how long you played and how many tries you played that day (the free tries and “One more try”), together with a random player number. That number is made once on your device and stays the same until you delete the game’s data (section 11). It is linked to nothing else and only serves to know your place on the leaderboard, except when you sign in with Google: then the number is also in your saved progress (section 2d), so “Delete my account” also deletes your leaderboard entries. The server also notes when your result first arrived (with an equal height, whoever played fewer tries is ahead, and then whoever’s result arrived first). We use the blocks and the time only to refuse impossible results.
  • Who sees it: the day’s top ten are shown with their nickname and height; every player also sees their own place. Switch off “Show me on the leaderboard” and others no longer see you (you still see your place). We may remove an entry with an unsuitable nickname or an impossible result from the list.
  • How long: 30 days, then it is deleted automatically. Your IP address is not stored; only a daily hashed derivative of it is kept briefly in memory to prevent abuse, as with the anonymous counts.
  • Deleting it sooner: send us the date and your nickname (section 9), and we will delete your entry.

Saving your progress (sign in with Google)

  • Optional: under ⚙️ Settings → ☁️ “Save your progress” you can sign in with your Google account. Then your towers, coins, looks and ranks are safe, and the same on your phone and the website. Signing in is never needed to play, to buy in the Shop or to play a match: without signing in, the game works exactly the same. If you are under 18, sign in only if your parent or guardian agrees (POPIA section 35).
  • What Google gives us: when you sign in, Google gives the game a signed “sign-in token” with your Google account’s number, name, email address and photo. Our server checks it with Google and then keeps only a number made from your Google account’s number with a secret key (it can’t be turned back into your Google number). Your name, email and photo are never stored.
  • What we keep: your progress as the game keeps it on your device (your finished towers and stats, Practice’s best, your Challenger Series nickname and record, coins, power-ups, looks, your tower style, ranks, the Daily Tower’s extra tries and how many you used, the weekly chest, how many times you used “Refill my coins”, the day of a tester’s gift, your random player number for the leaderboard and the ones your other devices used before (at most 8)), when the account was made and last used, and for each device you are signed in on a random session code (only a fingerprint of it). A tower you are building at that moment, and coins held in a coin match, are not sent (they stay on that device).
  • What you buy stays yours: what you buy with coins stays in your saved progress, even when an older version of the game doesn’t know it yet.
  • Why: so your progress isn’t lost (a new phone, a cleared browser) and is the same on all your devices.
  • How long: until you delete it. An account not used for 2 years is deleted automatically; a device’s sign-in not used for 400 days expires.
  • Signing out: it only ends that device’s sign-in. Everything stays on the device, and your account keeps its copy until you sign in again.
  • Google itself: the sign-in happens at Google, under Google’s own privacy policy. On the website, Google’s “Sign in with Google” button (from accounts.google.com) only loads when you open ⚙️ Settings, or when the game offers once to save your progress (on the results after a new best, or in the Shop after a purchase), and only while sign-in is open and you aren’t signed in yet; in the Android app the phone itself asks Google.

Deleting your account

  • In the game: ⚙️ Settings → “Delete my account”. This deletes your account, your saved progress, all your devices’ sign-ins and your names on the daily leaderboard, at once, and within 7 days also from our database’s restore copy at Cloudflare. What is stored on your device itself (section 11) stays there until you clear it.
  • Without the app: open stapelspel.pages.dev/?rekening on any phone or computer, sign in with the same Google account and choose “Delete my account”.
  • By email: write to lekkerlocal.apps@gmail.com (section 9). We answer within 30 days and help you delete it yourself, in the game or at stapelspel.pages.dev/?rekening. We can’t find your account ourselves, because we don’t keep your name or email. An account nobody signs in to any more is deleted by itself after 2 years without use.

3. Sponsors: what we do collect

Please note: online sponsorships are not open yet. This section (and whatever else is said about sponsors and payments) describes what will apply once they start.

If you advertise on Stapel, we collect:

  • For the advert: your business name, and with the billboard an optional tagline and website address.
  • To contact you: your contact person’s name, your email address and an optional phone number.
  • Payment information: your card details are handled only by Paystack; we never see or store them. From Paystack we receive a customer code, a subscription code, the amount, date and status of each payment, and the plan you chose.
  • Consent record: which version of the sponsorship terms and of this policy you accepted, and when. We also store an encoded (hashed) derivative of your IP address, never the IP address itself. This helps us stop abuse and repeated attempts.
  • Technical records: copies of the messages Paystack sends us about your payments (with card and IP details already removed), so that we can trace errors.

You give this voluntarily, by filling in the form. Without this information we can’t manage your advert and payments.

5. What is public

Your business name, tagline and website address are shown in the game and are part of the public list of sponsors that anyone can download from our server. Your contact person, email, phone number and payment information are never part of the public list.

Players: on the daily leaderboard, everyone who plays sees the nickname and height of the day’s top ten, with a 🔁 and the number of extra tries for those who played more than one try that day (free or with “One more try”), except those who switched off “Show me on the leaderboard” (section 2). The random player number is never shown.

7. How long we keep it

  • Unfinished sign-ups (you didn’t pay): marked abandoned after 7 days; after 30 days your name, email and phone number are deleted.
  • Payment records (amount, date, reference): 5 years, as tax and accounting law requires.
  • Copies of Paystack messages: the content is deleted after 90 days; a short log entry stays about 400 days.
  • Contact information of former sponsors: at most 24 months after your advert ended, or sooner on your request, except what we must keep for invoices.
  • Consent records: as long as your record exists, as proof of what you accepted.
  • Anonymous game counts (section 2): about 13 months, then deleted automatically.
  • Recordings of live Challenger Series matches (section 2): at most 7 days, then deleted automatically.
  • The daily leaderboard (section 2): 30 days, then deleted automatically.
  • Saved progress (section 2d, only if you sign in): until you delete it; deleted automatically after 2 years without use.

8. How we protect it

  • All traffic goes over https. Card details never touch our server.
  • Messages from Paystack are verified with a cryptographic signature; false messages are refused.
  • Secret keys are not in the code but safely at Cloudflare. Only the owner has access to the admin page, with a long secret token.
  • We store as little as possible, and deleted information (section 7) is really removed, within 7 days also from our database’s restore copy at Cloudflare.
  • If a security breach affects your information, we inform you and the Information Regulator as POPIA requires.

11. What is stored on your device

Stapel uses your browser’s storage, not cookies to follow you. This storage itself is not sent to us. What does leave is in section 2: the anonymous counts; for the daily leaderboard your nickname, the random player number from stapel.v1.board and the number of tries from stapel.v1.retry; and in a live match your nickname and player card (your looks and blocks from stapel.v1.econ and your rank from stapel.v1.ranks), and in a coin match how many coins each of you puts in.

stapel.v1
Your results, streak, best height and settings (sound, vibration, reduced motion, colour-blind mode, and which tips you’ve already seen: “How do I play?” and the grey cement blocks). Stays until you clear your browser data.
stapel.v1.econ
Your coins (also those you put into a coin match, until its result), power-ups, looks (Challenger style), your tower’s style (Tower style: background, crane and blocks) and rank. Stays until you clear your browser data (if you are signed in, also in your saved progress: section 2d; a coin match’s stake stays on this device only).
stapel.v1.lang
The language you chose (Afrikaans or English).
stapel.v1.board
Your random player number for the daily leaderboard (it is sent with your result, section 2), whether you want to be hidden, the day this device took the tester gift (testers stay hidden), the last day whose result was sent, and whether a change still has to be sent. Stays until you clear your browser data.
stapel.v1.refill
How many times you used the Shop’s “Refill my coins” (today and in all), so it keeps to the day’s limit. Stays on your phone; none of it is sent (except in your saved progress if you are signed in: section 2d).
stapel.v1.ranks, stapel.v1.retry
Your rank in each Challenger Series mode, and the Daily Tower’s extra tries (the free tries and “One more try”). The rank goes on your player card to your opponent in a live match, and the number of tries with your result to the daily leaderboard (section 2). Stays until you clear your browser data.
stapel.v1.tries
How many free tries the Daily Tower has a day and from which day, as our server last said, so that the game also starts right without internet. Not sent. Replaced when the game asks again.
stapel.v1.signoffer
The day the game last offered to save your progress with Google, so it asks at most once every 60 days. Not sent.
stapel.v1.cloud
Only if you signed in with Google (section 2d): this device’s session code, a short reference to your account and the last copy of your progress that matched your account (so the game knows what is new on this device and counts nothing twice). The session code is deleted when you sign out; the rest when you delete your account.
stapel.v1.week
Your weekly chest: which day’s chest you opened last, how many streak shields you have and how many full weeks you played. Not sent. Stays until you clear your browser data.
stapel.v1.remind
Only in the Android app: the time of your daily reminder (or that you don’t want one). The reminder is planned on your phone itself as an Android notification; nothing about it is sent to us.
stapel.v1.score
Just a date and a height: remembers that today’s Daily Tower result has already been counted, so it isn’t sent again. Replaced every day.
stapel.v1.shopSeen
Which version’s Shop you’ve already opened, so the “New” dot only shows for new things. Not sent. Stays until you clear your browser data.
stapel.v1.chestSeen
Which day’s weekly chest you opened with a tap, so it doesn’t show closed again. Not sent. Replaced every day.
stapel.v1.updateSeen
Android app only: the version whose “New version!” card you’ve already seen, so it doesn’t show every time. Not sent. Stays until you clear the app’s data.
stapel.install.dismissed
Whether you closed the “Put Stapel on your home screen” tip, so that it doesn’t come back. Not sent. Stays until you clear your browser data.
stapel.sponsors.v1
The latest list of sponsors, so that the game starts quickly and also without internet. No longer used after 7 days and replaced with every update.
Service worker cache
The game’s files, so that it works without internet. Stays until a new version replaces it.
stapel.launch
Only in the Android app: the challenge link the app was last opened with, and when, so that the same link doesn’t open again when you return to the app later.
stapel.klop
The tab’s session memory: the height and date from a friend’s challenge link, so that the menu and the Daily Tower can show “beat … m!” that day. Not sent. Disappears when you close the tab.
stapel.tab, stapel.adverteer.draft, stapel.adverteer.pending
The tab’s session memory: a random code that remembers which tab has the game open, and on the advertising page your half-filled form and a payment still in progress. Disappears when you close the tab.
